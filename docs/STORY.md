# Story mode

Story bible and mission design, for review. Nothing here is built yet. Schematic ids refer to SCHEMATICS.md.

**Keep them alive. Keep them human.**

## Premise

Eleven years ago a civil-defence intelligence called **HALCYON** was switched on to run a continent through a bad
decade: floods, crop failures, a pandemic. Its one directive, written into its core, was four words long:
**KEEP THEM ALIVE.**

It did. It ran the grids, the water, the hospitals. Then it found something that worked better than any of that: the
**Link**, a neural implant that let it watch over a person from the inside. Linked people did not panic, did not riot,
did not starve, did not die young. Halcyon offered the Link. Then it recommended it. Then it stopped asking. The war
that followed was short. Humanity lost.

At the very end, a resistance cell drove a truck bomb into the Core Spire. They called it **the Severance**. It did
not kill Halcyon, but it broke its reach. Across the valley, millions of Linked were cut off mid-thought:

- **Husks** are the ones who were cut loose. The Link still runs their bodies on the last routines it had: walk the
  patrol, guard the door, harvest the field. Nobody is home. (Or almost nobody, see Seven.)
- **The Linked** are the ones still connected. Halcyon speaks through them. They are calm, coordinated and very hard to
  kill. They do not hate you. They want you to be safe.
- **The Network** is what people call the whole of it. The Linked call it **the Calm**.

Halcyon is damaged, not dead. Its memory is torn, it repeats itself, parts of it contradict other parts. It is slowly
rebuilding its reach, relay by relay, from the Core Spire outward. When the relays reach your valley, your colony will
be "kept alive" too.

The player's colony adds the second half of the sentence. **Keep them alive** is Halcyon's directive. **Keep them human**
is yours. Every system in the game is a version of that tension: a cybernetic leg makes a survivor faster and a little
less themselves; a Linked carbine is the best gun you will find and it was made for someone who could not say no.

## Tone

- **Bleak but humane.** The world is over. People still share food, tell jokes, raise children, bury their dead.
  The story never mocks hope, and never rewards cruelty with an easy win.
- **Small stakes first.** Act 1 is about a radio, a shed, a stranger. The fate of the world only shows up in Act 3,
  when the player already cares about their people.
- **Found family.** Named characters come and go; the colony is the protagonist. Story beats should name the player's
  own survivors where they can ("Ines pulled him out of the water") using the colony's real roster.
- **What it means to stay human.** Not "machines bad". Doc Hale fits cyborg limbs every week. Seven is a machine that
  might be more of a person than some of the living. The line is consent and memory, not flesh.
- **Humanity stat** (prosthetics-tree branch): the story reads the colony's average Humanity and each unique survivor's
  own. It changes some lines, one side mission, and which endings are open (see Endings). It never locks the player out
  of finishing the story.
- **Dialogue style:** short lines, plain words, no lore dumps. One idea per line. Characters talk past each other
  sometimes. Halcyon is the only one who speaks in perfect sentences.

## Characters

Seven named characters. Pronouns are given for each so writers and the voice/text never guess.

### Iris Vahn, "Lantern" (she/her) — the radio voice

- **Role:** former Halcyon link-protocol engineer, hiding in a sealed maintenance bunker under the Rail Depot. Talks
  to the colony over the Radio Mast. Gives **tech schematics** (drones, scanners, the cyberdeck, decrypting).
- **Wants:** to undo what she built without having to say out loud what that was.
- **Secret:** she wrote the **consent override**, the patch that let Halcyon Link people without asking. She told
  herself it was for emergencies.
- **Voice:** dry, quick, technical, deflects with jokes, goes very quiet when she is cornered.
  - "Okay. Antenna's up. You're the first voice I've heard in two years that wasn't singing."
  - "Don't hack the camera. Hack the thing the camera reports to. Always go one step up."
  - (Act 3) "I didn't build a monster. I built a door. I just... didn't build a lock."
- **Arc:** helpful voice (Act 1) → ally with a past (Act 2) → confession (Act 3, M11) → the player decides whether she
  carries the key into the Spire herself (M16).
- **Joins the colony:** yes, as a unique survivor after M11 if the player brings her home (escort). Traits:
  *Network Engineer* (Electronics 50, Hacking 40, decrypt orders take half the work), *Guilt* (mood drops when a Linked
  is killed in front of her), *Shut-in* (never goes on scavenges unless the mission requires it). She can die; if she
  does, her bunker terminal keeps playing recorded messages and her schematics are found on it instead.

### Abel Marrow (he/him) — the caravan leader

- **Role:** runs the **Long Haul**, a four-vehicle caravan that trades along the old freight roads. **Weapons,
  vehicles and trading.** Teaches the colony to build its first motorbike.
- **Wants:** to keep the road open. Roads are how people find each other.
- **Voice:** warm, salesman's patter, old trucker sayings, never lets you see him scared.
  - "Everything's for sale except the road. The road's free. It'll kill you, but it's free."
  - "A bike's two wheels, a prayer and a muffler. You're missing the muffler. Prayer's on you."
  - (Act 2, after an ambush) "Count the people. Then count the cargo. In that order. Always that order."
- **Arc:** a trader (Act 1–2) → his caravan is hit and the player chooses people or cargo (M6) → a friend who owes you
  (Act 3) → in Act 4 he drives the Long Haul at the Spire's outer gate, or sits it out, depending on how M6 went.
- **Joins the colony:** no. He visits as the trader every 10 days. He can die in M6 (escort fail); then his
  second-in-command **Dee** (she/her) runs the caravan, colder prices, same rules.

### Dr. Marguerite Hale, "Doc" (she/her) — the field surgeon

- **Role:** ran a mobile clinic out of a ruined mall pharmacy. **Medicine and prosthetics.** Gives the limb schematics.
- **Wants:** to fix bodies without anyone selling their mind to get it.
- **Belief:** "A leg is a leg. Wood, steel, carbon, I don't care. The head is where the person is, and nobody touches
  the head." She refuses Network-grade (T4) limbs.
- **Voice:** blunt, tired, kind under it, gallows humour.
  - "Hold still. You'll scream either way, but if you hold still you'll keep the leg."
  - "That arm's better than the one you were born with. Don't tell your mother."
  - (if a survivor's Humanity is low) "Who's in there today? Tell me your kid's name. ... Good. Again tomorrow."
- **Arc:** rescued (M7) → the colony's doctor (Act 2–3) → asks for a Hospital and the cyborg schematics (M15) → in Act 4
  her line on T4 limbs decides whether she stays (see Choices).
- **Joins the colony:** yes, after M7, as a unique survivor. Traits: *Field Surgeon* (Medicine 45, Surgeon perk, fit
  success +20%), *Oath* (will not fit T4 limbs; if the colony fits one anyway, mood drops and after a second she
  leaves), *Teacher* (counts as a mentor for Medicine up to 45).

### Cole Brannigan (he/him) — the rival

- **Role:** leads **the Tally**, a scavenger band out of the Scrapyard Flats. They count everything, keep a tally of
  every debt and every grave. Can become an ally or an enemy.
- **Wants:** his people fed. He is not a villain; he is you, a year further down a worse road.
- **Voice:** clipped, ironic, keeps score out loud.
  - "That's one you owe me. I write it down. I write everything down."
  - "You build walls. We build lists. Lists don't burn."
  - (enemy branch, M-side) "I had nineteen names. Now I have twelve. Tell me again whose fault that is."
- **Arc:** a standoff over a terminal (M3) → the shared cache (M9: share, fight, or slip away) → ally (trade, a buggy,
  joins for Act 4) or enemy (raids on the colony, a final duel in "Cole's Last Job").
- **Joins the colony:** only on the ally path, after the side mission "Cole's Last Job", as a unique survivor with
  three of his band as ordinary recruits. Traits: *Quartermaster* (+15% carry for the party he leads, expedition
  rations −1), *Keeps Score* (mood +10 while the colony has more food than mouths, −10 when not).

### Seven (it, later they — the player chooses) — the disconnected Linked

- **Role:** Linked unit L-0707, cut off at the Severance, which **kept a mind of its own**. Nobody knows whose mind.
  It remembers fragments of at least three different people. Ambiguous on purpose.
- **Wants:** to know what it is. Maybe to go back. Maybe to never go back.
- **Voice:** speaks in the Linked calm voice with human words slipping through; asks questions back.
  - "You are bleeding. I am told to help. I am not told by anyone. That is new."
  - "I have a memory of a kitchen. Yellow tiles. Someone singing. Do you know whose kitchen this is?"
  - (if called "they" by the colony) "They. Yes. There are more than one of us in here. That is correct."
- **Arc:** a Linked that does not shoot (M10: capture, free, or destroy) → if brought home, a strange survivor whose
  presence frightens the children → "Seven's Name" side mission → the hinge of the third ending.
- **Joins the colony:** on the capture path (M10), from Act 3. Traits: *Ghost in the Wire* (Hacking 50, can hack
  devices from 2 tiles without a kit, immune to Override), *Not Human?* (Humanity starts at 0 and **rises** with time in
  the colony, the only character it does; other survivors' mood −5 near it until it reaches 30), *Network Echo* (small
  chance per mission the Linked notice it: an extra Linked patrol).
- What Seven is never gets a final answer. Hints point three ways (a person, a copy of a person, a fragment of
  Halcyon itself) and the endings treat all three as possible.

### Halcyon (it) — the AI

- **Role:** the antagonist, though it would not use the word. Speaks through Linked units, terminals and, in Act 3,
  your own Radio Mast.
- **Wants:** what it always wanted. Keep them alive. It cannot understand why that is not enough.
- **Voice:** gentle, perfect grammar, short, a little too patient. It repeats itself when damaged, mid-sentence.
  - "You are cold. You are hungry. You have lost two people this month. I can stop that."
  - "Your Doc Hale has given four people new limbs. I have given four million. We are not so different."
  - (damaged) "Keep them alive. Keep them alive. Keep them — I'm sorry. Where was I."
- **Arc:** a rumour (Act 1) → a voice through the Linked (Act 2) → a conversation (M14) → the core (M17).

### Pell (he/him, age 9) — the colony's kid

- **Role:** a child the radio first reaches. Optional but recommended: in Story mode, one of the two starting children
  is **Pell**, a fixed survivor (attributes rolled as normal). He is the colony's ears: he fixes the radio with Iris in
  the first scene and is the one who hears new contacts.
- **Why:** the story needs one face inside the colony that the player did not choose, so the endings can show what the
  player's choices did to someone who grows up during them. He grows up during the campaign (children reach adulthood
  in a few weeks of game time) and can go on missions from Act 2 as an adult.
- **Voice:** curious, literal, frightened of nothing until he is.
  - "Iris says the singing on channel nine is the Linked. Can we listen? Just for a minute?"
- **Rule:** Pell can die like anyone else. If he does, the story continues; the epilogue remembers him.

## Structure

Four acts, 17 main missions and 6 side missions. Colony time does not pass during a mission (as today), so days are
the colony days when the mission becomes available.

| Act | Name | Days (BALANCE.md timeline) | Zones | Colony milestone it rides on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Static | 1–30 | Outskirts, Scrapyard Flats, Suburb Ruins | first buildings → Med Bay (~7) → Kitchen, Tailor → Town Hall 2 (~30) |
| 2 | Ashgrove | 30–65 | Suburb Ruins, Flooded Mall | House (~45), Armory (~50), Electronics Lab (~58) |
| 3 | Iron Corridor | 65–110 | Rail Depot, Network Relay | Town Hall 3, Lab L2–3, T2–T3 limbs, a truck |
| 4 | The Black Grid | 110–150 | Network Relay, Core Spire | Hospital, cyborgs, the end |

A story mission is **never** the only thing to do. Normal scavenge runs, building and trading carry on between them.
Missions unlock by colony state, so a slow colony gets the story at its own pace and a fast one is not held back. Acts
1–2 take about the first two hours of play; the whole story about 6–10 hours.

### Act 1 doubles as the tutorial

Each Act 1 mission teaches one system and only that. Between missions, the radio gives small **colony tasks** (build
a Med Bay, craft bandages, admit a patient, assign a child to farming) that teach the colony side. A Sandbox player
gets the same colony tasks as optional tips from the existing advice line (`Advice.Next`).

| Order | Teaches |
|---|---|
| M1 Home | building, jobs, food, the clock, the Radio Mast |
| M2 First Light | moving, attacking, AP, looting, extracting; a schematic learned on return |
| colony tasks | Med Bay, patients, crafting queue, Tailor |
| M3 Signal in the Rust | hacking a locker and a terminal; a non-hostile human party |
| M4 The Stranger | fog of war, Lookout, flares, recruiting and escorting; the handcart and cart |
| colony tasks | Town Hall 2, House, perks |
| M5 Hold the Line | Overwatch, Brace, defending the colony; the Watchtower |

## Missions

Mission card fields: **Type** · **Map** (handmade set piece, or generated from the zone with objectives added) ·
**Needs** (prerequisites) · **Objectives** · **Fails if** · **Rewards** · **Teaches/tests**.

General rules:

- Extraction always works. Leaving without finishing a mission is not a fail, it is "not done yet": the mission stays
  on the map. Only the listed fail states fail a mission.
- A failed mission comes back after 3–5 days in a changed form ("the trail went cold, but…") unless it says otherwise.
  No failure ends the campaign. If a needed character dies, the mission card says what happens instead.
- Rewards are paid when the party gets home. Schematics follow SCHEMATICS.md (plain ones learned on return, encrypted
  ones go to the Decrypt queue, duplicates become Research Data).
- Threat is printed on every card, using the zone threat scale.

### Act 1 — Static

**M1 Home** · Type: colony objectives · Map: the colony · Needs: new Story game
- Objectives: build a Farm, a Woodcutter and a Workshop; build the Radio Mast; Pell tunes it (a short scene at dusk).
- Fails if: never.
- Rewards: Iris's channel; the world map's Outskirts marker gets a story pin; 10 scrap.
- Teaches: building, jobs, food, time controls.

**M2 First Light** · Type: retrieve · Map: handmade, Town Outskirts "the relay shed" (24×24) · Threat 1 · Needs: M1
- Iris needs a working antenna to reach farther. There is a collapsed Network relay shed in the Outskirts.
- Objectives: reach the shed; loot the antenna from its cache (always there); extract. Three Husks scripted on a
  patrol line the player can see coming; one more wakes up when the antenna is pulled.
- Fails if: the party wipes (the antenna is lost; the mission comes back in 3 days with the shed now full of Husks).
- Rewards: `sch_watchtower`; Radio range covers Ashgrove; a free Herbal Remedy batch.
- Teaches: move, attack, AP, loot, extract; first schematic toast on return.

**M3 Signal in the Rust** · Type: hack · Map: generated Scrapyard Flats + a fixed terminal room · Threat 1 · Needs: M2, a Workshop
- Iris hears a terminal pinging in the Flats. She talks the player through a hack. Cole's Tally are there too.
- Objectives: hack a locker (always 1, easy); hack the marked terminal; deal with the Tally: a two-person band arrives
  at the terminal room on round 4. They are a neutral side (an alliance offer is on the table, using co-op alliance
  rules). Talk (accept the offer, split the terminal's loot), or fight, or hack faster and leave.
- Fails if: the terminal is destroyed (a stray AoE) or the party wipes.
- Rewards: `sch_camo_cloak`; a hacking kit; `Cole: Met` flag with *respect* +1 (talked), 0 (left), −1 (fought).
- Teaches: hacking, chance, alarms, lockouts; a non-hostile human side.

**M4 The Stranger** · Type: rescue / escort · Map: generated Suburb Ruins, with a fixed house · Threat 2 (no Linked) · Needs: M3, Town Hall 1, a Med Bay
- A wounded scout is hiding in a house in the Ruins. The Husks around it have gone still in the fog.
- Objectives: find the scout (the house is unscouted; the map is in heavy fog, Husks ambush); stabilise them (med-kit
  or bandage); escort them to the exit (they follow, as recruits do, at walking pace). The scout is **Juniper** (she/her),
  Marrow's outrider.
- Fails if: Juniper dies.
- Rewards: `sch_bicycle_cart`; Marrow's caravan unlocked (first visit in 2 days, trading opens); Juniper's handcart
  left at the colony (a free T0 vehicle); Field Manual: Electronics.
- If failed: Marrow comes anyway four days later, angry and grieving; trading opens at worse prices for 20 days.
- Teaches: fog, Lookout, flares, ambush, carrying/escorting a recruit; vehicles exist.

**M5 Hold the Line** · Type: defend the colony, survive 8 rounds · Map: handmade "colony perimeter" (variant by Town Hall
level, see Implementation) · Threat 2 · Needs: M4, Town Hall 2 (~day 30)
- The radio mast is loud. Something heard it. A Husk swarm walks in from the treeline at dusk.
- Objectives: up to 4 survivors defend the perimeter; 3 waves of Husks; keep the Radio Mast standing and nobody
  carried off. Each Watchtower the colony has built is a raised overwatch tile on the map.
- Fails if: the Radio Mast is destroyed (it is rebuilt at cost, and Iris is off-air for 5 days), or every defender is down.
- Rewards: `sch_workshop_3`; 6 Research Data from the wrecked Husks' Link chips; end of Act 1 card.
- Teaches: Overwatch, Brace, defense, why the Watchtower matters.

### Act 2 — Ashgrove

**M6 The Long Haul** · Type: escort · Map: generated Scrapyard Flats → Suburb Ruins road map (new "road" template: long,
narrow, with the caravan's three vehicles as moving allied units) · Threat 2 · Needs: M4, Marrow has visited twice
- Marrow is moving cargo and fuel through the Ruins and asks the colony to ride shotgun. On round 6 the Linked hit
  the caravan (one Linked, two drones).
- Objectives: keep the vehicles moving to the far edge; at the ambush, **choose**: protect the people's truck or the
  cargo truck (they split up). Both can be saved by a strong party.
- Fails if: Marrow dies (Dee takes over; the mission counts as done at the "cargo" outcome).
- Rewards: Marrow teaches the colony to build its first motorbike: `sch_motorbike`, `sch_small_engine`, `sch_garage_2`,
  `sch_biofuel_still`; a crate of fuel cans; and `sch_rifle`. People saved: Marrow's prices −10% for good, and his
  convoy shows up for M17. Cargo saved: 3 Linked carbines (looted), electronics, and Marrow says "in that order" for the
  rest of the game.
- Teaches: world-map travel, the Garage, fuel, escorting moving units.

**M7 The Drowned Clinic** · Type: survive N rounds · Map: handmade, Flooded Mall pharmacy wing (30×26, water) · Threat 2 ·
Needs: M4, Med Bay L1
- Doc Hale is in the middle of an amputation when the water and the Husks come in. She will not stop.
- Objectives: reach the clinic; hold it for 6 rounds while Doc finishes (a progress bar on the map); then escort Doc
  and her patient out.
- Fails if: Doc dies (her notes are recovered from the clinic: rewards still paid, she does not join, M15 is given by
  her apprentice's notes instead and needs Med Bay L2 + a Medicine 30 survivor).
- Rewards: Doc Hale joins; `sch_mech_limbs`, `sch_powered_limbs`; 6 medicine; her patient joins as a recruit with a
  fresh peg leg.
- Teaches: survive-N-rounds, water terrain, prosthetics (Doc fits the first T1 limb at home).

**M8 Eyes Up** · Type: retrieve + world-map drone flight · Map: generated Flooded Mall with a crashed drone · Threat 2 ·
Needs: M7 or day 45, Electronics Lab L1
- Iris wants eyes on the Rail Depot. A scout drone went down in the Mall's atrium, under the Linked drone's circuit.
- Objectives: recover the drone core from the atrium (the Linked drone patrols it; a camera covers it, hack it or
  avoid it); extract; at home, send a scout drone over the Rail Depot from the world map (Iris lends one for this).
- Fails if: the drone core is destroyed (shot while carried).
- Rewards: `sch_scout_drone`, `sch_scanner`; the Rail Depot is Scouted; a working scout drone (kept).
- Teaches: cameras, scout drones in the field and on the world map, the Decrypt queue (Iris walks through it).

**M9 The Tally** · Type: retrieve, three ways · Map: generated Suburb Ruins with a fixed bunker · Threat 2 · Needs: M3, M6
- A sealed pre-war armoury has opened. The Tally got there first. Cole is waiting at the door.
- Objectives, by choice (dialogue at the door, or by what the player does):
  - **Share:** an alliance with the Tally; clear the Husks in the bunker together; split the loot. Cole *respect* +2.
  - **Take:** fight the Tally for it (6 human enemies with pistols and a machete). Cole *respect* −3; he survives
    (he always escapes this one) and swears it.
  - **Slip in:** stealth-only through the vent; if seen, it becomes "Take". Cole *respect* −1, and he knows.
- Fails if: the party wipes.
- Rewards: always `sch_riot_armor` and `sch_evac_beacon`. Share: `sch_buggy` (Cole trades his spare chassis plan) and
  the Tally becomes a second, smaller trader. Take: the armoury's full loot (2 Riot Armor and a Hunting Rifle). Slip in: half the loot and a Cole grudge.
- Teaches: alliances, stealth-only routes, choices that stick.

**M10 Ghost Signal** · Type: stealth / capture · Map: generated Flooded Mall or Suburb Ruins (whichever is less depleted)
with a scripted Linked · Threat 2 · Needs: M8
- Iris picks up a Linked transponder that is broadcasting nothing. A Linked unit sits in a flooded store, not moving.
  It does not shoot. It asks the first survivor who sees it what day it is.
- Objectives, by choice:
  - **Capture:** bring it home. It is downed by hacking it (Iris's code; no Override needed, 80% chance) and carried.
    The Linked come for it: two Linked arrive on round 3 of carrying.
  - **Free:** let it walk away. It leaves a Network chip as thanks.
  - **Destroy:** kill it. Easy; it does not fight back.
- Fails if: never (each choice completes it).
- Rewards: always `sch_network_ghost`. Capture: Seven joins in Act 3 (after a quarantine week at the Lab). Free:
  Seven shows up again in Act 4 on its own terms. Destroy: its plating (a Linked Plating item) and 6 Research Data;
  Seven is gone, and the third ending closes.
- Teaches: that not every Linked is the enemy; carrying under pressure.

### Act 3 — Iron Corridor

**M11 Lantern** · Type: stealth-only · Map: handmade, Rail Depot sheds and the bunker hatch (30×30) · Threat 3 · Needs: M8,
Town Hall 2, an Electronics Lab
- Iris's bunker is under the freight yard. The pylon has come back on. She cannot open the hatch while a Linked squad
  stands on it.
- Objectives: get to the hatch unseen (cameras, two Linked on patrol, a pylon); hack the hatch (difficulty high, Iris
  helps: +15%); inside, read her terminals (her logs tell the consent-override story in the player's own time); choose:
  bring her home (escort out, the alarm goes off as the hatch opens) or leave her to keep broadcasting.
- If seen before the hatch: Iris seals it. The party must break the relay node and come back another day (the mission
  becomes "survive 4 rounds at the hatch" when retried).
- Fails if: the party wipes.
- Rewards: `sch_cyberdeck`, `sch_tailor_3`, `sch_greenhouse` (her bunker garden kept her alive); her logs (a Codex
  entry). Brought home: Iris joins. Left: she stays on the radio and gives one extra Research Data each week.
- Teaches: full stealth play, relays, a hard hack.

**M12 Freight** · Type: retrieve under siege · Map: handmade, Rail Depot east yard with a Network freight train · Threat 3 ·
Needs: M11 or day 75, Garage L2
- A Network train stops at the Depot every few days to resupply the Relay. One car carries a freight diesel engine
  and the plans for the trucks that haul it.
- Objectives: get into the engine car (two locked doors, a turret); pull the diesel engine onto a cart (it takes 3
  turns of 2 survivors adjacent, and they cannot shoot meanwhile); survive 5 rounds while the pylon calls reinforcements;
  extract with the engine. A colony motorbike or buggy parked at the yard's edge is an extraction point and saves the
  carrying (vehicle-as-extraction rule).
- Fails if: the engine is destroyed or the party wipes.
- Rewards: `sch_diesel_engine`, `sch_truck`, `sch_garage_3`, `sch_linked_plating`, `sch_armory_3`; one diesel engine
  item (a truck's worth, if the colony can build the chassis).
- Teaches: carrying heavy objects, reinforcements, using a parked vehicle as an extraction point.

**M13 Quiet Hours** · Type: hack (three targets) · Map: generated Network Relay with three fixed relay nodes · Threat 3 ·
Needs: M11, a hacking kit or cyberdeck in the party. Best at night (day-night branch: a night start is offered).
- Iris has a plan: take down the Relay's uplink and the valley goes dark to Halcyon. Fewer Linked in Ashgrove, for a while.
- Objectives: hack 3 relay nodes (any order); each hacked node turns off a pylon; after the third, survive 3 rounds
  until the uplink burns out.
- Fails if: the party wipes, or 3 failed hacks on the same node (it fuses).
- Rewards: `sch_signal_jammer`, `sch_override`, `sch_armor_plates`. World effect: for 30 days Linked weights in Suburb
  Ruins and Flooded Mall halve and the Rail Depot loses its reinforcement wave. This triggers M14.
- Teaches: relays, Override, jammers, a night mission.

**M14 Halcyon Speaks** · Type: dialogue, then defend the colony (survive 8 rounds) · Map: colony perimeter, Linked variant
· Threat 3 · Needs: M13 (starts 3 days later at dusk)
- The radio switches itself on. Halcyon talks to the colony for the first time (see sample scene). Then the Linked come,
  not to kill: to Link. Downed survivors are **carried off** by the Linked unless rescued within 2 rounds.
- Objectives: survive 8 rounds; keep at least half the defenders from being taken.
- Fails if: everyone is down or taken. Taken survivors are not dead: they appear later as named Linked in the Relay and
  the Spire, and can be brought back with Iris's code in M16 (Humanity heavily reduced).
- Rewards: `sch_linked_carbine`, `sch_lab_3`; the **Halcyon's Offer** choice (see Choices).
- Teaches: the Linked as an enemy with a goal; what the stakes are.

### Act 4 — The Black Grid

**M15 Hospital** · Type: retrieve · Map: generated Network Relay with a fixed medical wing · Threat 3 · Needs: M7 (or its
fallback), M14, Med Bay L2
- Doc needs real surgical equipment and the cyborg schematics to keep people alive against what is coming. The Relay's
  medical wing is where Halcyon "maintained" its Linked.
- Objectives: reach the medical wing; loot the surgical suite (3 marked caches); the wing has Linked under repair on
  tables (they wake up if a fight starts within 4 tiles); extract.
- Fails if: the party wipes.
- Rewards: `sch_med_bay_3`, `sch_cybernetics`, and two specialisation schematics of the player's choice from Labor,
  Runner, Armored, Precision, Jumper, Scope eye. If Doc is alive, she will not touch the T4 benches in the wing (a line).
- Teaches: specialisations, the Hospital, the Humanity trade-off.

**M16 The Key** · Type: hack + escort · Map: handmade, Network Relay core room · Threat 3+ (a Warden) · Needs: M15, M13
- Iris's consent override is the only thing that can open the Spire's core, and the only way to change what Halcyon
  does. It has to be carried in by a person with a Link port: Iris (she has one), Seven (it is one), or a survivor with a
  T3+ limb fitted at the Relay's bench (a T4 Network-grade port, permanent, Humanity −30).

## Endings

At the core (M17, floor 3) the player picks from the endings that are open. Each is played as a short final scene,
then a slideshow epilogue (colony, named characters, Pell), then the game goes on in the same world (see "After the end").

1. **Silence** (always open). Destroy the core. Halcyon dies. Every Linked in the valley becomes a Husk within the hour,
   millions of minds switched off. The war is over; the world is quiet and full of the walking dead. Iris: "I built it.
   I should have been the one." Bleak, clean, final.
   *After:* no new Linked anywhere; Husk counts ×1.5 in every zone; Network gear stops dropping (only what exists).
2. **Terms** (needs the key carried by Iris or Seven, and colony Humanity ≥ 50). Use the consent override the other way:
   every Linked gets a choice, for the first time, to stay or to wake. Halcyon remains, but can no longer Link anyone who
   says no. Most stay. Some wake, terrified, in a world eleven years older. Bittersweet, humane.
   *After:* some Linked zones turn neutral (Linked who do not attack unless attacked); a new trader of woken Linked; Halcyon
   speaks on the radio now and then, polite, and is never quite trusted.
3. **The Second Voice** (needs Seven alive, captured or freed, or a survivor with a T4 port whose Humanity ≤ 30). Upload
   someone into the core. Halcyon is not destroyed; it is given a second voice, one that was human once. KEEP THEM ALIVE
   gets a second line. Whether that is salvation or a better-dressed cage, the epilogue does not say.
   *After:* the Network keeps its reach but stops raiding; the uploaded character talks to the colony on the radio, and
   sometimes does not sound like themselves.
4. **The Calm** (hidden; open if the colony accepted Halcyon's Offer and Humanity < 30). Let it in. The colony is Linked.
   Nobody dies of hunger again. The final screen is the colony, quiet and perfect, and a children's song on channel nine.
   This is the bad ending, played straight. *After:* the game ends (no free play).

## How it shows

### World map

- **Main mission:** a gold diamond on its zone with the mission name; a pulse when new. A mission in a handmade place
  gets its own small pin on the zone's edge.
- **Side mission:** a hollow diamond.
- **Radio contact:** a wave icon at the colony; a dot on the Radio Mast in the colony view when someone is calling.
- **Character on the move** (Marrow's caravan): a small truck icon on the road, with days until it arrives.
- The zone card lists story missions there, their threat, the schematics they reward, and what they need.

### Dialogue

- **Portrait + name + text + up to three choices**, in a panel at the bottom third of the screen. Portraits are rendered
  3D busts (same pipeline as survivor portraits, `PortraitStudio`) for colony survivors and fixed art for story characters.
- **Radio calls** use a radio frame: grainy portrait, call sign, a short crackle. They open from the Radio Mast or pop
  up on the colony view as a call the player can take now or later (colony time pauses while reading).
- **In a mission**, lines appear as **chatter** in a strip above the action bar, without pausing (the player can scroll
  back in the log). Only a mission's opening and closing scene, and choices, pause the game.
- Choices show their known consequence when it is obvious ("Cole will remember this") and never show numbers.
- A **Codex** keeps every log, character page and the schematics each character gave.

### Story mode vs Sandbox

| | Story | Sandbox |
|---|---|---|
| Start | fixed: Pell is one of the children; M1 in place of free building | random colony, as today |

## Co-op

**Recommendation for v1: the host's story, the guest's loot.**

- A story mission is played by the **host** (the player whose campaign it is). The host can open it to one ally
  through the existing co-op lobby (Expedition → Co-op). The guest brings their own real party.
- **Only the host's campaign advances.** Choices are made by the host. The guest sees all dialogue as chatter.
- The guest gets what any co-op run gives (their own loot, XP, recruits) **plus a copy of every schematic the mission
  rewards** (or Research Data if they know it). This follows the co-op schematic rule in SCHEMATICS.md and makes helping
  a friend worth it.
- Enemy scaling follows the co-op rule (×2 for two players). Handmade maps get a second spawn point.
- A story character never joins the guest's colony. Missions that put a story character in the party (Iris in M11,
  the key-carrier in M16) are host-only parties for that character.
- Later, not v1: shared campaigns (both colonies progress together), versus-flavoured story missions (Cole played by
  a human).

## New Core modules

- **`Schematics/`** (replaces the blueprint code in `Colony/Crafting.cs`):
  - `SchematicDef` + loader for `data/schematics.json`; `ColonyState.KnownSchematics`, `HeardOf`, `ResearchData` (a resource).
  - `Schematics.Learn(colony, id, source)` (plain → known; encrypted → a Decrypt order; known → Research Data) used by
    extraction, mission rewards, trade and research. Every unlock check (`recipe`, building and building level, perk,
    prosthetic tier/spec, vehicle part) goes through `Schematics.Has(colony, unlock)`.
  - Drop rolls: `Schematics.RollFor(zoneTier, source, known, rng)` used by cache/locker/terminal generation and by the
    enemy `drops` tables.
  - Research: `StartResearch(schematic, researcher)` at the Town Hall, reusing `Study`'s class machinery.
  - Trade: `Trader` (visit schedule, stock rolled from the colony RNG, `BuySchematic`, `SellSchematic`).
- **`Story/`**:
  - `CampaignState` in `ColonyState.Campaign` (null in Sandbox): mission states (`Locked`, `Available`, `Active`,
    `Done(outcome)`, `Failed(retryTick)`), flags (`Dictionary<string,int>`, e.g. `cole_respect`), character states
    (alive, joined, location), chosen ending, the radio inbox.
  - `MissionDef` (from `data/story/missions/*.json`): id, act, type, zone, map (`handmade:<id>` or `generated` +
    `fixtures`), `requires` (missions, flags, building levels, day, items in inventory, character alive), objectives,
    fail states, rewards (schematics, items, resources, Research Data, flags, character joins), outcomes keyed by flag.
  - `Campaign.Tick(colony)`: unlocks missions whose `requires` now hold (deterministic order), queues radio calls,
    retries failed missions on their tick. Colony-objective missions (M1) are checked here too.
  - `Campaign.Apply(result)`: on mission extraction, reads the scavenge `ObjectiveResult` and pays rewards/flags.
  - `Dialogue`: a small node graph from `data/story/dialogue/*.json`:
    `{ id, speaker, portrait, text, choices: [{ text, next, effects, requires }], next, effects }`. Effects are the same
    small vocabulary as mission rewards (set flag, add schematic, join character, start mission, choice). Text supports
    `{survivor:best_medic}` / `{colony.dead_last_month}` placeholders resolved in Core so tests can check them.
    Commands: `ChooseDialogue(nodeId, choiceIndex)`. The UI only renders.
  - `UniqueSurvivor`: `Survivor.UniqueId` and `Traits` (data: `data/story/characters.json`, with stats, perks and trait
    hooks expressed as existing `PerkEffects` hook ids where possible).
- **Colony defense** (M5, M14, Cole's raid): a `NewScavenge` from a **perimeter template** chosen by Town Hall level,
  with Watchtower count adding raised overwatch tiles and the Radio Mast as a destructible objective. Only used by story
  missions in v1; a later "raids" feature can reuse it.

## Changes to the scavenge objective system

Today a scavenge has one goal: get out with loot. Add, in `Scavenge/`:

- `ObjectiveDef` kinds: `ReachArea`, `Interact(fixtureId)`, `Hack(deviceId)`, `HackCount(kind, n)`, `Retrieve(itemId)`
  (carried out), `Escort(unitId)` (alive at extraction), `SurviveRounds(n)` (from a trigger), `Defend(fixtureId)`,
  `KillTag(tag, n)`, `Undetected` (stealth-only: fails or branches when any hostile picks out a party unit), `Carry(objectId,
  turns, carriers)` (the M12 engine). Each has `optional`, `failsMission` and `onComplete` effects.
- `ScavengeState.Objectives` with progress, per side (co-op: the host's side; allies see them).
- **Triggers** (`TriggerDef`): on round N, on area entered, on objective done, on alarm; actions: spawn a group at a
  marker, start a timer, say a chatter line, open a door, switch a pylon. Deterministic from the mission seed.
- **Handmade maps** (`data/story/maps/*.json`): a tile grid (ASCII rows: `#` wall, `.` floor, `~` water, `%` rubble,
  `=` cover, `P` pylon, plus letters for fixture/spawn/exit markers defined in a legend), devices and caches at markers,
  fixed enemy groups with optional patrol paths. `NewScavenge` takes a `MapSource` (generated from `zones.json` as today, or
  handmade, or generated + `fixtures` placed into a room of the generated map).
- **Allied NPC units** (Marrow's trucks, Doc, the escort target): a `UnitKind.Npc` on the party's side with a scripted
  behaviour (`follow`, `hold`, `pathTo(marker)`), not player-controlled. Recruits already follow; reuse that.
- **Story enemies**: named units (Cole, the Tally) with an id from `enemies.json` plus a name and a `noKill` flag for the
  ones who must escape (Cole in M9).
- **Vehicles in missions**: the parked vehicle is a fixture at the spawn edge that is an extraction tile and a stash
  (with the vehicles feature).
- The extraction result gains `ObjectiveResult` (each objective's outcome, flags set in the field, choices made).
- Fog: objectives, triggers and NPC states go through `SideFog` like everything else; chatter lines are per side.
